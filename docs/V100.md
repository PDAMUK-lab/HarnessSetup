# The optional V100 tier

Two Tesla V100 SXM2 cards, each on its own SXM2-to-PCIe adapter, inside the Windows desktop. They run a second `llama-server`
(CUDA) on their own port, so a bigger model sits **entirely in graphics memory**, beside the RX 6600 XT server that already runs.

**It is optional and dormant by default.** Nothing in the kit changes until `V100_ENABLED=1`, so you can buy and build the hardware
later and switch the software on when it is ready. This page is the whole path: what to buy, how to build it, the install order,
and what to do when it misbehaves.

> **Not verified on real hardware.** The software was written and tested without a V100 in reach: everything that can run on a
> test machine is tested (settings, scripts, estimates, dry runs), and the facts were checked against NVIDIA's and llama.cpp's own
> sources (October 2026). The physical side rests on build logs from other people, nearly all of them on Linux. Treat the first
> install as a commissioning exercise, one card at a time, and use `Check-V100.ps1` at every step.

## Is it worth it?

| You get | You pay |
| --- | --- |
| The strongest model the kit knows (Qwen3.8-27B) fully in graphics memory, at interactive speed: on one 32 GB V100 the same architecture measured about 34 tokens/s at 4K context and 16 at 128K, and the built-in MTP head adds roughly 1.3 to 1.5 times. The 8 GB RX 6600 XT can only run that model overnight. | **Idle power:** about 40 W per module, around the clock (roughly 730 kWh a year for two). |
| The day model keeps running on the RX 6600 XT; the V100s are an extra endpoint, not a replacement. | **Power supply:** plan for 1000 W or more with correctly labelled 12 V outputs (about 760 W sustained with both cards capped at 200 W, about 960 W uncapped). |
| `Desktop-Mode.ps1 away` frees all of it whenever you need the PC. | **Noise and heat:** SXM2 modules have no fan of their own. Adapter fans can be loud (one builder measured 82 dB at full speed) until you wire them to a motherboard fan header. |
| | **A legacy driver:** the V100 needs the R580 data-center driver, NVIDIA's last branch for Volta (supported to June 2028). Newer branches dropped the card. |

## What to buy

* **Two single-module adapter cards**, each carrying one SXM2 module on a PCIe x16 edge connector. No bifurcation, no dual-module
  boards. Two single cards cannot be linked with NVLink; the kit splits the model by layers over plain PCIe, which is what it
  needs (layer split sends very little between the cards).
* **Check the module:** it must be a **V100 SXM2**. SXM3 (DGX-2) and SXM4 (A100) modules do not fit or run on SXM2 adapters, and some
  listings blur this. Ask for a photo of the module label and the exact name (`V100-SXM2-16GB` or `-32GB`).
* **16 GB or 32 GB?** Both work. At 128K context two 16 GB cards take Qwen3.8-27B at `UD-Q4_K_XL`; two 32 GB cards take
  `UD-Q6_K_XL`. The listing must say which; `Check-V100.ps1` shows the real size once installed.
* **Ask the seller, in writing:** the 12 V connector standard (EPS "CPU" 8-pin or PCIe 8-pin) and how many leads per card; what
  heatsink and fan come in the box (and whether the fan has a 4-pin PWM plug); a photo of the board.
* **A power supply with enough 12 V outputs of the right kind.** An EPS 8-pin and a PCIe 8-pin look almost the same but are wired
  differently, and mixing them can short the supply. Use only the cables that came with that exact PSU model, in the sockets
  labelled for them; never splitters, Molex or SATA adapters, or a "PCIe to EPS" adapter. Count the connectors before you buy.

## Building it

1. **Power off, switch the PSU off and unplug it.** Nothing here is hot-pluggable. Touch bare chassis metal, hold boards by the edges.
2. **The RX 6600 XT stays in the main x16 slot** with the monitor attached. Put the V100 adapters in the other slots. On most
   desktop boards the extra x16-sized slots are fed by the chipset at x4 (or share lanes with an M.2 socket): that is plenty for
   generation (the cards are PCIe Gen3 devices and little crosses the bus), it only makes loading the model slower. The RX 6600 XT
   itself is an x8 card, so it loses nothing even if the board splits its slot x8/x8. Your board manual has the lane-sharing table.
3. **Cooling is your job.** The module is passive (300 W peak). Make sure each heatsink has an airflow path, plug the fan into a
   motherboard header (not a fixed 12 V pin), and fit the whole stack in the case or use an open frame. Support the adapter by its
   bracket: the SXM2 connectors are not made for repeated re-seating or for carrying the weight of a heatsink.
4. **Install one V100 first**, boot, run `.\Check-V100.ps1`, then add the second.
5. **BIOS / UEFI:** Above 4G Decoding **on**; CSM **off** (UEFI boot only); Resizable BAR on if the board has it (turn it off as a
   troubleshooting step if the PC will not boot with all three cards); primary display = the slot with the RX 6600 XT;
   PCIe slot speed Auto (set Gen3 if the link is unstable). Two 32 GB cards plus the Radeon need a lot of 64-bit address space:
   an older board may simply not POST.
6. **Windows must boot in UEFI mode** (`msinfo32` shows BIOS Mode UEFI, and the system disk is GPT). Convert with Microsoft's
   `mbr2gpt` first if it is not.
7. **Turn off Windows Fast Startup** (Power Options > Choose what the power buttons do), so Shut down is a true cold boot. A
   V100 can fail to come back after a warm reboot until the PC is fully powered off.
8. After the first sustained run, power off and look at the connector bodies and cables at the PSU and the adapters for heat or
   discoloration.

## Installing the software (the order)

Run on the desktop in an **administrator** PowerShell, in the kit's `desktop\windows` folder.

1. `.\Check-V100.ps1`: does Windows see the cards? No settings and no administrator rights needed. A Device Manager code 12 means
   the BIOS cannot map the cards (Above 4G Decoding), 43 and 10 mean a power, cooling or driver fault.
2. **The driver:** install the **Data Center driver 582.78 (R580)** from NVIDIA (Data Center / Tesla > V-Series > Tesla V100 >
   Windows 10/11), **not** a GeForce driver and **not R590 or newer**, which no longer contain the V100.
   `.\Install-V100.ps1 -DownloadDriver` fetches the pinned installer and checks NVIDIA's signature; you run it yourself.
3. Settings: `.\Configure.ps1 -Only V100_ENABLED` (it then asks for the card count and size and the quantization). On the laptop
   run `./setup.sh configure --only V100_ENABLED` too, or copy `config/node.env` across: both machines must agree on
   `V100_ENABLED`, `V100_PORT`, `V100_MODEL_ALIAS` and `V100_CTX`.
4. `.\Install-V100.ps1`. It switches the cards to TCC mode (a compute-only driver mode) and stops: **reboot, then run it again.**
   The second run installs the CUDA 12 build of llama.cpp in `C:\llama-cuda`, checks it lists both cards, estimates whether the
   model fits **before** downloading it, downloads the model, writes the start script, the laptop-only firewall rule and the
   `llama-v100` task, starts the server and runs the tool-call smoke test. The first start is slow: the driver compiles the CUDA
   code for the V100 once.
5. On the laptop: `./setup.sh run 13` (opens the port in the firewall), then `./setup.sh tool v100-laptop`. It adds the
   `desktop-v100` endpoint to Hermes and puts it first among the local ones (`V100_PRIMARY=0` puts it second).
6. `./setup.sh tool verify`, and `.\Check-V100.ps1` again on the desktop.

Re-running `Install-V100.ps1` is safe. `-UpdateLlama` replaces the CUDA build, `-SkipModelDownload` and `-NoStart` do what they say,
`-DryRun` shows everything without changing anything.

## Settings

All are asked only when `V100_ENABLED=1`. Change one with `.\Configure.ps1 -Only KEY` (desktop) or `./setup.sh configure --only KEY` (laptop).

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `V100_ENABLED` | `0` | The switch. Off means the kit behaves exactly as before. |
| `V100_COUNT` | `2` | Cards installed. |
| `V100_VRAM_GB` | `16` | Memory per card (16 or 32). Used for the fit check. |
| `V100_QUANT` | `UD-Q4_K_XL` | Quantization of Qwen3.8-27B (file name and download URL follow it). |
| `V100_MODEL_ALIAS`, `_FILE`, `_URL` | derived | Name Hermes uses, file in `C:\models`, download address. Override them to run another model. |
| `V100_CTX` | `131072` | Context size (at least 65536). |
| `V100_PORT` | `8081` | The V100 server's port. Must differ from `LLM_PORT`. Only the laptop may reach it. |
| `V100_PRIMARY` | `1` | The V100 model comes first among the local endpoints. |
| `V100_MTP` | `1` | Use the model's built-in multi-token-prediction head (only if the file has one; the installer checks). |
| `V100_SPLIT_MODE` | `layer` | `layer` (safe) or `tensor` (experimental). |
| `V100_DRIVER_MODE` | `TCC` | `TCC` (compute only) or `MCDM` (only if you need the cards in WSL2). WDDM is not possible: graphics on a data-center card needs NVIDIA's vGPU licence. |
| `V100_POWER_LIMIT_W` | `200` | Per card; `0` leaves the stock limit. Never set above what the card reports as its default. |
| `V100_CUDA_DIR` | `C:\llama-cuda` | Where the CUDA build lives, separate from the Vulkan build in `C:\llama`. |

## Which model fits

At 128K context, one request at a time, estimated with the same formula `Install-V100.ps1` uses (the busiest card must have 1 GiB
to spare):

| Cards | Qwen3.8-27B | Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (alternative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 x 16 GB | `UD-Q4_K_XL` (17.6 GB). `UD-Q5_K_XL` is tight with MTP; fine without it (`V100_MTP=0`). | `UD-Q4_K_XL` (22.4 GB) with an f16 cache |
| 1 x 32 GB | `UD-Q5_K_XL` (20.9 GB) | up to `UD-Q5_K_XL` (26.6 GB) |
| 2 x 32 GB | `UD-Q6_K_XL` (25.3 GB); `UD-Q8_K_XL` fits but gains little | up to `UD-Q8_0` |
| 1 x 16 GB | does not fit | does not fit |

File sizes are what Hugging Face reports today and have changed before; the installer reads the live size. To run the 35B-A3B
instead, set `V100_MODEL_ALIAS`, `V100_MODEL_FILE` and `V100_MODEL_URL` (its MTP head is only in the separate `-MTP-GGUF` repo).
The 35B-A3B is much faster (it activates 3B parameters per token); the 27B is the stronger model.

## Using it

* `hermes-mode status` (laptop) shows the V100 endpoint beside the others; `hermes-mode local` uses the local profile.
* The V100 model comes first in the local profile and in the fallback chain: OpenRouter, then V100, then the desktop's day model, then the laptop.
* **Taking the desktop away** (any time you need the PC for something else): see "Desktop away" in the runbook. `.\Desktop-Mode.ps1 away`
  stops every desktop model server and tells the laptop to leave the desktop out; `back` reverses it.

## Tuning

* **Power limit.** Generation is limited by memory bandwidth and rarely needs more than about 150 W, but reading a long prompt can
  pull 220 to 240 W per card, so a 200 W cap can slow prompt processing somewhat. Raise `V100_POWER_LIMIT_W` only with a strong
  PSU and proven cooling; lower it (150 to 175) if a card stays above about 80 C. NVIDIA's throttle points for the PCIe V100 are
  87 C (slowdown) and 90 C (shutdown); read your module's with `nvidia-smi -q -d TEMPERATURE`.
* **Layer or tensor split.** Layer split is the default and tolerates the slow link between the cards. Tensor split is
  experimental: on a dense 27B one builder measured much faster generation (about 20 to 70 percent on two NVLinked cards) and slower prompt reading;
  your two cards are not NVLinked, so measure before you trust it: stop the server and run
  `llama-bench.exe -m <model> -ngl 99 -fa on -sm layer` and again with `-sm tensor`, then set `V100_SPLIT_MODE` and re-run the installer.
  Tensor mode needs an unquantized (f16) context cache and turns MTP off.
* **MTP.** Faster generation for about 1.3 GB more memory. Turn off with `V100_MTP=0` if the server runs out of memory.
* **CUDA graphs** are switched off for layer split (a memory growth was reported on exactly this hardware). If you want to try them,
  delete the `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS` line from `start-llama-v100.cmd` (the installer rewrites that file, so make the change after the last install).
* **Context.** Longer than 128K needs more memory than two 16 GB cards have; the installer warns.

## When something is wrong

| Symptom | Likely cause and fix |
| --- | --- |
| PC does not POST with the cards in | Above 4G Decoding off, CSM on, a direct-slot adapter that does not train its link (try another slot, force Gen3, or a cable-type adapter), or a power cable fault. Install one card at a time. |
| `Check-V100.ps1`: "Windows sees 0 cards" | Seating, 12 V cables, BIOS. Power off completely (cold boot) and try again. |
| Device Manager code 12 | BIOS cannot map the cards: Above 4G Decoding on, CSM off. |
| Device Manager code 43 / 10 | Power or cooling fault, or the wrong driver (R590 or newer, or a GeForce driver). |
| `nvidia-smi` is missing | No data-center driver. `.\Install-V100.ps1 -DownloadDriver`. |
| A card is missing after a restart | Warm-reboot dropout: shut down fully and start again; turn off Fast Startup. |
| llama.cpp lists 0 CUDA devices | Release builds fail silently: the CUDA runtime DLLs (`cudart64_12.dll`, `cublas64_12.dll`, `cublasLt64_12.dll`) must sit next to `llama-server.exe`, and the driver must be the R580 data-center driver. |
| "no kernel image is available" | A CUDA 13 build. Only the CUDA 12 build has Volta code; the installer picks it. |
| Out of memory while loading | Smaller `V100_QUANT`, shorter `V100_CTX`, or `V100_MTP=0`. |
| First start takes many minutes | Normal once: the driver compiles the CUDA code for the V100. Do not stop it. |
| A card runs hot or the PC is loud | Fan on a PWM header, airflow, lower `V100_POWER_LIMIT_W`. |
| The Vulkan day server uses the V100s | The installer hides NVIDIA's Vulkan driver from it and checks; `Check-V100.ps1` re-checks. If it still sees them, add `--device Vulkan<N>` (the RX 6600 XT's number from `llama-server.exe --list-devices`) to `C:\llama\start-llama.cmd`. |
| The laptop cannot reach the V100 server | `./setup.sh run 13` on the laptop (firewall), and the V100 firewall rule on the desktop (`Check-V100.ps1`). |

## Not verified

* Everything physical: your adapter's power connector, cooler and fit, whether your board maps three GPUs, real temperatures.
* Real speeds on two separate PCIe cards (the published numbers are for one card, or for NVLinked cards).
* Whether `nvidia-smi -dm` on an SXM2 module behaves as documented, and where a TCC card appears in Device Manager.
* Windows PowerShell 5.1 (the scripts are tested on PowerShell 7).
* The open llama.cpp reports about CUDA on Volta (a memory growth with CUDA graphs, launch errors on some Qwen3.5-family MoE models,
  long prompts on many cards) may or may not affect your build. Keep `-np 1`, test one long prompt after installing, and see the
  table above if something fails.
